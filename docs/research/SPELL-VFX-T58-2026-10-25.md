# T58 spell VFX toward key art S1, 2026-10-25

**Outcome.** VISUAL-PLAN (b) shortfall 3 said: "Magic is untextured primitives: a flat cyan or orange sphere, no
droplets, sparks, glow or trail." That is now closed in greybox, on the existing recycled sphere:

- **Bolt:** the opaque unlit core is unchanged and keeps its exact threat colour. Around it sits a soft falloff shell in
  the same colour, with a short school trail behind: six sagging droplets for Water, eight rising, flickering embers for
  Fire, four winding wisps for Air (grit for Earth, which has no caster).
- **Absorb:** when the ward takes a bolt, the bolt goes into a splash crown in its own colour, with ten droplets thrown
  out across the ward (embers rise, droplets fall).
- **Perfect absorb:** the same splash, plus a widening ring of 20 beads at the palm and a short flash at its centre.
- **Unblockable telegraph:** the red rim pulses (1.6 Hz, wider and brighter at the rim's own chroma). It now sits under
  the black body, so the "leave" cue draws as a black core with a red rim again (section 3.3: before T58 the rim
  covered the core, and the cue drew as a plain red disc).
- **Steel (dodge) is untouched and never glows.**

**Cost.** One shared translucent material (`M_SpellGlow`). All the puffs of one colour are drawn by one instanced sphere
layer, so the whole effect costs one draw call per glow colour on screen, whatever the projectile count. D-G4 shows
two colours (hound fire, maw water): **+2 draw calls**. The headroom left for T57 and the braziers is **23-25 calls**
(125-127 of 150 at the max), against 25-26 before.

**Colour language.** Every glow colour is `SpellVfx::GlowColour` of the look the presentation already drew the threat
with. The rim pulse is `SpellVfx::RimPulseColour` of the rim. `MageArena.SpellVfx.ColoursKeepThreatClass` holds each
one to its class by chromaticity, under the greybox palette and a moved palette. The existing threat-colour tests
(`MageArena.Colour.*`, `MageArena.Greybox.ThreatColours`, `ThreatPairReserved`) and `Greybox/ThreatDemo.*` are unchanged
and green.

**The material** (`M_SpellGlow`): unlit, translucent (alpha blend), two-sided, `bUsedWithInstancedStaticMeshes`.
Emissive = `Color`; Opacity = `saturate(pow(saturate(-dot(N, V)), GlowFalloff 1.5) x GlowGain 1.0)`. Only the far
hemisphere of a sphere shows, densest at its centre and fading to nothing at its silhouette. A bolt's opaque core stands
in front of that half and keeps its exact colour (section 3.2), and a blend can only move a pixel toward exactly the
threat colour (section 3.3). Forward shading, the tonemapper and the exposure are untouched.

**Built on what was there, no parallel system.** The shot keeps its recycled sphere (`ShotFor`) and its colour from
`ElementLook`. The material comes from the existing budget FX commandlet. The ward events are the kernel's own
(`perfect`, `hit`, `Metrics.Blocks`). `Greybox::MakeInstances`, `Greybox::SharedTint` and `FGreyboxTintCache` are
reused. No Niagara component and no mesh section is added per projectile.

## 3. Decisions the card did not specify

1. **Instanced puffs, not Niagara.** A shared Niagara system would need a data channel or array interface to spawn at
   100 moving projectiles, and it could not be pinned in a frozen still. One instanced layer per colour is one draw
   call, deterministic, and testable without an RHI. The cost: no per-puff alpha, so a puff fades by shrinking.
2. **The far-hemisphere material.** The first material lit the front of the sphere, and the shell added over the core:
   the fire core went yellow in the first after stills, a colour-class risk. Lighting only the far half lets the
   opaque core hide the glow inside its own silhouette, so the core's pixel is exactly the threat colour, as before.
3. **Alpha blend, not additive.** Two blind critics read the additive glow drifting over the bright sand and sky: fire
   toward yellow-gold, air toward lime. The cause was the sum clipping (sand + fire clips R and lifts G). An alpha blend
   moves a pixel from the background toward exactly the threat colour and never past it. The third critic, on the
   blended stills, flagged no glow drift. The price is a softer glow with no "hot" core, and less contrast for fire on
   the orange sand (section 5). The perfect burst also dropped its crown disc and centre flash, which stacked under the
   ring.
4. **The unblockable rim.** In every before still the rim cylinder (`At + 1 cm`, 2.2 x the radius, 4 cm thick) sat over
   the 5 cm body and hid it: the leave cue drew as a plain red disc, with no black core. With T58 on, the rim sits 1.5 cm
   below the body, so the core shows, and its pulse stays opaque. Additive red glow beads were tried first. Over the
   ochre sand they read salmon, toward the fire class, so they were dropped (the before frames in section 5 show it as it was).
5. **The struck bolt leaves no ghost** when the ward takes it (the 0.28 s ghost stays for every other ending), so the
   splash is not hidden behind an opaque sphere in front of the palm.
6. **Fire is the palette's ember orange (linear 0.90, 0.26, 0.02), not the style block's vermilion #FF3B1F.** The brief
   says the colours are read from `Session/ElementColour.*` and never re-picked. ElementColour moved fire away from
   vermilion in T26 because vermilion sat next to the unblockable rim red, so T58 keeps ember orange.
7. **Puffs count against the particle budget** as if each were a particle, capped at 500 (1,468 + 500 <= 2,000).
8. **The report is this doc, not `runs/T58/REPORT.md`:** `runs/` is never committed (repo law), and the brief names this
   path.

## 6. Open points

- **The proxies.** The six `NS_Budget_*` systems stand in for VFX that T58 now draws for real. Dropping some of them
  from the stress scene would give back particles, not draw calls. That decision belongs to the budget owner, not to
  this card.
- **Toward S1:** a denser, finer spray (more, smaller droplets) costs puffs, not draw calls. There is room under the 500
  cap in the duel, but not in D-G4 (400 used). Fire contrast on the sand could come from a darker ember rim, still in
  the fire class. Both are follow-ups, not in this card.
- **The trail of a bolt flying at the eye** is foreshortened behind its shell, so it reads mostly as a glow. It shows
  in profile (the Air wisps, the hound embers in D-G4). A camera-facing streak would read better head-on, and it is a
  follow-up if the critic or the owner asks for it.
- **The unblockable lane** (the Tempest Lance line, the Leviathan Orb path) pulses the same way. Neither was posed.
- **Quest truth:** like every D-G4 number, these are desktop proxies. Additive overdraw from 400 small puffs is not
  measured on device.
