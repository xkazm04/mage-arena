## 1. What changed (lever 1 only, the atlas cells)

| Cell | T67 | T72 (final, round 3) |
|---|---|---|
| Flame A and B (4, 5) | backing flame 1.08x the body, shade 0.40-0.46, hard edge | backing = the body's own flame grown evenly by 0.1, shade 0.24 at a soft edge (alpha ramp 0.03) grading to 0.30-0.36; heart scale 0.46 (T67 0.4) |
| Flame star (7) | outer star 0.46-0.82 | a backing star grown by 0.07 at 0.24-0.30 under it; heart burst radius 0.2 (T67 0.1) |
| Sparks (in 4-7, 14) | rim to 0.5, halo 0.55 | rim band 0.26, halo 0.22 |
| Spark streak (14) | dark tail 0.3 wide at 0.46 | thin dark band 0.27 wide at 0.28 |

`flameLayer` and `sdfLayer` take a `grow` (an even dilation of the signed distance), and `flameLayer` takes a `soft` (the
alpha ramp width). Edge-band shade in the atlas (mean of opaque pixels 1-3 px from the silhouette, and 4-6 px in): flame
0.469 / 0.520 -> 0.298 / 0.376, star 0.587 / 0.694 -> 0.308 / 0.437, streak 0.567 -> 0.375. Every cell stays a grey mask
(R = G = B) with shade at most 1. The heart is the full element colour (255), and its share of the opaque pixels holds:
0.098 / 0.096 / 0.097 (T67's test needs 0.08).

**Why the hue looks brown.** The material draws Emissive = threat colour x shade. A darker shade of vermilion is, in sRGB,
a burnt umber. The critics' suggestion of "a deep red-orange rim, not brown" needs a second hue, which the mask cannot
give (the mask is grey). A second hue would be a new per-instance colour (a 4th custom float) or a material change. Both
are outside this card: custom data stays three floats, and the hue stays the threat colour.

## 2. The value contrast, measured

`tools/fire-contrast.py`, on each still. The footprint is the saturated fire pixels (HSV S > 0.66, hue under 45 deg) in a
fixed region of interest. The **edge band** is the footprint's pixels within 3 px of its outline, only where the outline
borders the sand. The **sand** is the sand-coloured pixels 4-12 px outside it, below the sand line. Luminance is relative
luminance of linearised sRGB, and the contrast is Weber, (median edge - median sand) / median sand. The bolt is posed
3.5 m out, so its crop is scaled by 3.5/8 first, to its size at the 8 m duel ring. The absorb and the perfect happen at
the ward (1.1 m) by design and are measured at native size. A no-VFX plate (`-MageArenaSpellVfx=0`, `T72-plate/`) was
captured as a reference, but it draws T58's old orange splash sphere, so the saturation footprint was used instead.

| Shot | Before (T67) edge / sand / contrast | Round 1 | Round 2 | Final (round 3) edge / sand / contrast |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 04 fire bolt, 8 m size | 0.184 / 0.303 / **-0.39** | -0.66 | -0.59 | 0.123 / 0.294 / **-0.58** |
| 05 fire absorb | 0.194 / 0.276 / **-0.30** | -0.59 | -0.54 | 0.128 / 0.277 / **-0.54** |
| 06 fire perfect | 0.227 / 0.272 / **-0.17** | -0.46 | -0.45 | 0.128 / 0.232 / **-0.45** |

The fire's full colour (0.9, 0.26, 0.02) already has about the sand's luminance, so the silhouette now sits on a darker
edge: the contrast is negative and about 1.5x (bolt) to 2.6x (ring) T67's.

## 4. The blind critic

Pairs come from `capture-spell-vfx.ps1` (all 11 shots), each pair a half-size full frame plus a crop. The bolt and stone
crops are scaled to the 8 m size and then shown x2 nearest. A and B are shuffled with seeds 7201-7203, and the key is
kept in `critic-N-key/`. Each round a fresh agent saw only the images and S1. It was asked about (a) value separation
from the sand, (b) colour class (white core, the unblockable's black core and red rim, class drift) and (c) whether any
school got worse.

Round 1 (flat 0.2 hard outline) read "a sticker, a bit muddy" and the ring "a thorn wreath". Round 2 graded and softened
the rim, slimmed the star backing and eased the streak tail; the ring still read "rope or a thorny vine". Round 3 made
the streak's dark band thin. All three rounds, unblinded:

- **Separation:** every round reads it, on every fire pair. That is the goal of the card.
- **Class safety:** no white or yellow-white core anywhere, and nothing reads as the unblockable. All three critics say
  so: "the darkest fire edges are brown-orange, never near-black, so the leave threat stays unique."
- **The cost:** every round names a drift from vermilion toward brown, rust or rope, worst on the perfect ring. In
  round 3 the before frame was closer to S1 on all three fire pairs (MINOR). This is fire looking less like S1's fire,
  not a class break, but it is a "worse for its school" call on the fire pairs, and it is reported as such.
- **Water, Air, the unblockable and the stone:** their cells and code did not change. The water-bolt and unblockable
  differences come from the capture, not the change: the shell flicker phase and the rim's four-step pulse land
  differently between the one before capture and the after captures.

## 6. Open points for Athena

- **The trade-off is hers.** The value contrast she asked for is real: it is measured (about 1.5-2.6x T67's
  edge-to-sand step) and read by every critic round. But a darker vermilion is umber, and the critics read it as brown,
  rust or rope and the before as closer to S1. Options:
  - (A) Accept T72 as it stands: separation at 8 m over S1 likeness.
  - (B) Keep T72's flame and star rims, and return the spark streak (the perfect ring) to T67's: the ring is where the
    rope read is strongest.
  - (C) Revert to T67, and open a card for a second rim hue: a 4th custom float or a material rim term carrying a
    deeper red-orange. This changes the custom-data shape, and her colour-language rules must allow a rim hue that is
    not the threat colour.
  - Recommendation: (B) if separation stays the priority at 8 m; (C) if S1 likeness is.
- **Quest truth** is unmeasured: ASTC on the 0.24-0.36 band, and whether the soft rim survives the Quest mips at 8 m.
- **The steel stone** reads "pale, near off-white" to all three critics, in both frames. It is untouched here and
  outside this card; it may need a darker steel in the palette (a colour-language call).
- **The no-VFX plate** (`-MageArenaSpellVfx=0`) is not a clean plate: it draws T58's splash sphere. A plate switch that
  hides every glow would make this measure exact. That is a capture-tool change outside this card's paths.
